Hae In Park Ph.D.

Applied Linguist

Associate Professor and Chair
Department of Applied English Linguistics and Translation Studies
Kyung Hee University

In a globalized world, acquiring an additional language has become an ordinary part of human experience, and a consequential one. My research asks how additional languages are learned, how they reshape the speakers who learn them, what bilingualism and multilingualism afford, and how inquiry into these questions may be conducted with the methodological rigor they require.

01 / Research

Research

Four strands, running from how bilingualism reshapes cognition to whether the instruments the field relies on can support the claims built with them.

Instrument

Elicited imitation and proficiency measurement

Whether the elicited imitation task can carry the weight placed on it. Parallel form equivalence, rater and scoring effects, performance with heritage speakers, the contribution of working memory, and current work on automated scoring and an EIT response corpus.

SSLA · Modern Language Journal · RMAL
Cognition

Bilingualism and event construal

How Korean–English bilinguals conceptualize motion. Cross-linguistic and cross-cultural work on agentivity in caused motion events, the encoding of complex motion, and the conditions under which a second language shifts nonverbal categorization.

Bilingualism: Language and Cognition · Lingua · Frontiers in Psychology
Practice

Measurement practice in SLA

How the field reports proficiency and why it matters. Survey and replication work on researcher beliefs, reporting conventions, and whether results from different studies can meaningfully be compared at all.

Language Learning · SSLA
Beliefs

Beliefs about language and its users

What learners and speakers take bilingualism to be worth. Native speakerism in evaluations of teacher legitimacy and accent, monolingual assumptions about bilingual competence, and the perceived benefits of learning an additional language.

Modern English Education · work in progress
